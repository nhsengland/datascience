# 📃 Agreed Commission

INLINE

*A formal commission provides legitimacy and clarity for project scope, authority, and accountability. It helps ensure we are keeping our efforts aligned to organisational priorities.*

**Success Criteria:** The project has a documented, approved commission from the appropriate authority.

* **RED:** No formal commission has been approved or recorded.
* **AMBER:** Commission approved but not documented, or approval incomplete. Not clear that commission is (or continues to be) aligned to current organisational prioritisation.
* **GREEN:** Formal commission approved by all relevant parties and documented in full.

## Good Practice Guidance

| MSC | Good Practice |
| --- | --- |
| MUST | * Your project must have a named Commissioner who is aware of the project and their role as Commissioner.   *Note: For smaller informal projects, or where work is primarily for Professional Development purposes, it might be that your line manager is the appropriate commissioner.* |
| MUST | * Formal commission / endorsement / approval of the work is recorded. |
| SHOULD | * Project appears in relevant D&A / TD project tracking and corporate reporting - e.g. the Book of Work. |
| SHOULD | * Alignment of the project to the appropriate D&A Pillar / Foundation has been agreed by the relevant Pillar / Foundation owner. |

**Possible Evidence:**

Use your main project page or a stakeholders page to name the commissioner.
