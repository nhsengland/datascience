## Legal basis

In NHS England/NHS Digital context, “Direction,” “Mandate,” and “Other” refer to the formal legal mechanisms that give us the authority or requirement to process or obtain certain datasets. Where a legal basis exists, we should reference them on our IG template.

### **1. Direction**

- **What it is:**  
  A **Direction** is a legally binding instruction from the Secretary of State for Health and Social Care (or equivalent authority) to NHS Digital, now NHS England to collect, process, and/or share specific information.  
  It is usually made under powers in the Health and Social Care Act 2012. As of **1 February 2023:**The statutory functions of NHS Digital transferred to NHS England under the [Health and Social Care Information Centre (Transfer of Functions, Abolition and Transitional Provisions) Regulations 2023](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2023/98/contents/made) (Transfer Regulations) with effect from 1st February 2023 (Transfer Date).
- **What it does:**  
  It tells that we _must_ do something – for example, collect a specific dataset from all NHS trusts, or share data with another public body for a defined purpose.
- **Examples:**
  - _The [COVID-19 Public Health Directions 2020](https://digital.nhs.uk/about-nhs-digital/corporate-information-and-documents/directions-and-data-provision-notices/secretary-of-state-directions/covid-19-public-health-directions-2020)_ required NHS Digital to collect and share data to support the pandemic response.
  - [OpenSAFELY Data Analytics Service](https://digital.nhs.uk/about-nhs-digital/corporate-information-and-documents/directions-and-data-provision-notices/data-provision-notices-dpns/opensafely-data-analytics-service?utm_source=chatgpt.com) Data Provision, requires NHS England to establish and manage a system for accessing pseudonymised GP data to support audit, evaluation, research, and public health planning.

- **How to know if it applies:**
  - Check the **[Directions and Data Provision Notices](https://digital.nhs.uk/about-nhs-digital/corporate-information-and-documents/directions-and-data-provision-notices)** page on NHS Digital’s site and [Data Provision Notices](https://digital.nhs.uk/about-nhs-digital/corporate-information-and-documents/directions-and-data-provision-notices/data-provision-notices-dpns?utm_source=chatgpt.com) on NHS England's site
  - Look for a Direction that names the dataset or purpose you are working with.
  - If the dataset you are using was collected under a Direction, that Direction is part of your legal basis for using it.
  - Often, your IG or data access approval pack will reference the relevant Direction if it applies.

---

### **2. Mandate**

- **What it is:**  
  A **Mandate** is usually the annual or periodic _NHS Mandate_ from the Department of Health and Social Care to NHS England.  
  It sets out NHS England’s objectives and responsibilities – including specific duties that require collecting or using data.
- **What it does:**  
  It provides the high-level policy and strategic basis for activities, including those involving data.
- **Examples:**
  - [Road to recovery: the government's 2025 mandate to NHS England](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/road-to-recovery-the-governments-2025-mandate-to-nhs-england/road-to-recovery-the-governments-2025-mandate-to-nhs-england).
  - The [_NHS Mandate 2023–2024_](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/nhs-mandate-2023/the-governments-2023-mandate-to-nhs-england) includes "cut NHS waiting lists and recover performance" which could underpin certain data projects.

- **How to know if it applies:**
  - If your project is directly delivering on an NHS Mandate objective, you can cite it.
  - These are often relevant for high-level strategic initiatives or system-wide programmes (e.g., elective recovery, integrated care).
  - Mandates tend to be broad, so you’ll usually still need a more specific Direction, DPN, or other legal basis for the actual data processing.

## Governance & Compliance

### **1. UK GDPR Principles - [A guide to data protection principles](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-protection-principles/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-principles/)**

2. Purpose limitation
3. Data minimisation
4. Accuracy
5. Storage limitation
6. Integrity and confidentiality (security)
7. Accountability

---

### **2. Mandatory Training**

All project members should have completed the mandatory IG training through ESR so that they understand their responsibilities around data protection and reporting breaches etc.

---

### **3. Caldicott Principles**

These principles apply to the use of confidential information within health and social care organisations and when such information is shared with other organisations and between individuals, both for individual care and for other purposes. There are eight principles which can be found in detail [here](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-caldicott-principles).

**Principle 1:** Justify the purpose(s) for using confidential information

**Principle 2:** Use confidential information only when it is necessary

**Principle 3:** Use the minimum necessary confidential information

**Principle 4:** Access to confidential information should be on a strict need-to-know basis

**Principle 5:** Everyone with access to confidential information should be aware of their responsibilities

**Principle 6:** Comply with the law

**Principle 7:** The duty to share information for individual care is as important as the duty to protect patient confidentiality

**Principle 8:** Inform patients and service users about how their confidential information is used

---

### **4. National Data Opt-out**

National Data Opt-out is usually documented as part of the DPIA, stating whether it applies to the data in question. If it does not apply then the DPIA should state the reason for this exemption.

---

### **5. Additional Approvals**

Some projects, especially those supporting decision making or integrated into live systems/products maybe require additional approvals such as Health Research Authority (HRA) or The Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA).
