# Data Hazards - How to run an assessment

This space provides a shared overview of how we assess, discuss, and monitor potential data-related risks across our data science projects. These risks or **data hazards** are issues that can arise when working with data, algorithms, or automation. They include concerns such as algorithmic bias, lack of consent, environmental impact, and the risk of misuse.

It is the ethical responsibility of every project team to assess data hazards because the decisions we make when designing, building, and deploying data-driven systems can have real-world consequences - including reinforcing inequality, harming vulnerable groups, or eroding public trust. By proactively identifying and discussing potential harms, we uphold our duty to design responsible, fair, and transparent systems. Ethical oversight is not the sole domain of governance or compliance teams; it belongs to everyone involved in shaping how data is used. Running a data hazards assessment ensures that we act with awareness and accountability, embedding ethics into the core of our technical work rather than treating it as an afterthought.  
  
  
  
**What is a Data Hazards Assessment?**  
  
A data hazards assessment is a structured group session run by someone fromEthics held with individual project teams in the Data Science team.  
  
The assessment works like this:  
• One by one, each potential hazard is presented on screen along with a clear explanation of what it is and why it matters.  
• After each hazard is introduced, the team is given about a minute to silently reflect on whether and how that hazard applies to their project.  
• At the end of that time, all participants **independently rate the hazard** as High, Medium, or Low risk.  
• This leads into a **facilitated discussion**, where differing ratings are explored and team members share their perspectives. This is often the most valuable part of the session - it surfaces risks, trade-offs, and context that may not have been obvious to all.  
• After the discussion, the group agrees on a final risk level and documents any relevant mitigations or proposed work.  
  
This process is repeated for all hazards in the framework.

**What does High / Medium / Low risk mean?**  
Each hazard in this template should be assessed based on how it applies to your specific project. The risk rating reflects your team’s judgement of both the **likelihood** that the hazard could occur and the **impact** it would have if it did.  
• **High risk** means this hazard is very likely in your project or could have serious consequences if it occurred.  
• **Medium risk** means the hazard is possible and could have moderate consequences.  
• **Low risk** means the hazard is unlikely to occur or would have minimal impact.  
  
There is no “objective” right answer - what’s important is that the rating reflects your project’s real risks and supports a meaningful discussion during the assessment.   
  
  
  
**How to Record Your Assessment**  
  
Once the assessment is completed, the agreed ratings and notes should be recorded in a data hazards log using our shared template.  
• You can access the **[Data Hazards Template](https://nhsd-confluence.digital.nhs.uk/display/DAT/DS_xxx:+Data+Hazards+Assessment:+Project+Name+-+Template)** here to copy and work on it in your project space.

• Once copied and filled in, your project will automatically appear on the **[Data Hazards Monitoring Dashboard](./Data%20Hazards%20Monitoring%20Dashboard.md)** with its current status.

Step by step instructions to fill in the Data Hazards Template:Step 1: Replicate the Template for your project

1. Open the **[Data Hazards Assessment template](https://nhsd-confluence.digital.nhs.uk/display/DAT/DS_xxx:+Data+Hazards+Assessment:+Project+Name+-+Template)**  
2. Click the **••• menu** (top right) and select **Copy**.  
3. Rename the page to match your project, e.g. **DS\_246** – Data Hazards Assessment: **Ethical AI**. (recommended naming convention)  
4. This should be automatic, but make sure the page is saved with the label: data\_hazard\_monitoring

Step 2: Complete the Data Hazards Log Table using the Hazards Assessment session

1. Use the copied template to fill in the hazard log table. The Data Hazards assessment sessions can guide this.  
2. For each hazard, use the data hazards assessment sessions to select or fill in:  
  
• **Risk Level** (High / Medium / Low)  
• **Description** of the risk as applicable to the project  
• **Mitigation** plans or actions to mitigate the risk  
• **Last Reviewed** is date when hazard was last reviewed  
  
**Tip**: All label options are included with descriptions about what they mean, just delete the ones that don’t apply.

Once filled, your project will **automatically appear** on the [monitoring page](./Data%20Hazards%20Monitoring%20Dashboard.md). Any changes in the status of these hazards can then be updated using the respective data hazards page created for the project.

**When to use the Data Hazards Log:**

* Review applicable hazards when you have an idea of what your project involves; the idea being that an early review gives you the vocabulary to spot hazards further into the project.
* Review often, especially when scope/direction changes.
* Update the log when any mitigating actions are complete.

**How Data Hazards should be handled throughout a project lifecycle:**

* **Start of the project**

The hazards should allow us to assess where the 'danger zones lie' , so attention can be directed to where potential harms could appear in the project and be dealt with early on - it is easier to embed ethical considerations at the start of the project than it is to retroactively add them when you are looking at an end product.

* **During the project**

The hazards should be referred back to throughout the project's development to ensure ethical review is not a one-off exercise.

Ideally, re-visiting the data hazards log should be done with a wide variety of stakeholders a various points throughout the project's lifecycle.

* **End of the project**

The hazards should provide a 'health warning' label which we can apply to our end products to communicate potential harms in simple accessible terms.
