# 🪜 Proportionate QA

_Different projects require different levels of quality assurance depending on their complexity, risk, resource availability, and the criticality of the decision(s) they will support. Determining the right level of QA at the outset ensures proportionality and prevents over- or under-assurance._

**Success Criteria:** The level of QA required has been assessed and agreed, proportionate to project complexity, risk and impact. Requirements for compliance with any additional assurance processes (e.g. [Business Critical Models](https://nhs.sharepoint.com/sites/X24_DataAnalytics/SitePages/Business%20Critical%20Models.aspx)) have been identified.

- **RED:** No consideration has been given to level of QA required and/or level of planned QA is clearly insufficient or overly burdensome.
- **AMBER:** The planned level of QA is determined and appears appropriate for this project stage, but decision making may not be documented. May not have considered the full lifecycle of the project, including any need for compliance with relevant external standards / authorities.
- **GREEN:** The planned level of QA is appropriate based on the complexity, resources, risk and time associated with the project. There has been consideration of requirements for later stages of product lifecycle, including with respect to external standards / authorities where relevant.

The level of quality assurance must be proportional to the complexity of the project and the impact of the project. The consequences can range from financial loss, to reputational damages and legal challenges. You should consider whether the project is high-profile and/or critical to achieve certain political/organisational goals. These types of projects may get specific scrutiny from senior executives or ministers, extra media attention, or even have specific legislation. Also consider if the project is potentially contentious or relates to 'hot' political issues - for example, handling of personal primary care data, gender identity, eligibility for NHS care for different communities.

### **Key standards all projects must consider**

- If your project involves creating a model or any significant analysis, it must be submitted to the [Business Critical Model register](https://nhs.sharepoint.com/sites/X24_DataAnalytics/SitePages/Business%20Critical%20Models.aspx) as soon as possible, regardless of your triage assessment. If deemed Business Critical, you will need to complete and submit the [QA Review Framework template](https://nhs.sharepoint.com/:x:/r/sites/ModelQualityAssurance/Shared/NHSE%20Model%20QA%20Template.xlsm).
- Any outputs that will have a clinical impact must undergo a clinical assurance process. You will also need to consider whether the output is classified as a [medical device](https://www.hra.nhs.uk/planning-and-improving-research/research-planning/how-were-supporting-data-driven-technology/overview-legal-requirements-using-health-and-care-data-development-and-deployment-data-driven-technologies/4-checklist-standards-codes-and-approvals/#three) and will need to be regulated by the MHRA.
- The [Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard (ATRS) template](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/algorithmic-transparency-recording-standard-hub) should be completed for any algorithmic tools which:
  - Have a significant influence on a decision-making process with public effect
  - Directly interact with the general public

**Possible Evidence:**

- completed Business Critical Models triage template.
- record of conclusions from following the MHRA SaaMD flow chart.
- record of conclusions from reviewing ATRS scope requirements.
- note of discussion with your AQA reviewer.
