> Our team recently organised a joint conference bringing together data scientists from NHS England, the Department of Health and Social Care, and NHS Business Services Authority. This blog covers some of the highlights from a fantastic two days!

<!-- more -->

**On Tuesday 20th and Wednesday 21st October, data scientists from across NHSE, DHSC and NHSBSA came together in Leeds for two days of presentations, workshops and collaboration, celebrating the innovative data science work taking place across health and care.**

![THbLgtsw](../images/blogs_images/2026-joint-conference/1f8aeb26-496b-44f2-9fe4-0d7670402ae8.jpg)

## Day One: Celebrating the Data Science Accelerator

Tuesday kicked off with the Data Science Accelerator (DSA) Celebration Event. The DSA is a collaborative initiative between healthcare organisations; a 12-week programme aimed at data professionals and enthusiasts across DHSC, NHS England and NHSBSA.

Participants are paired with experienced mentors, dedicating one day per week to a data science project tied to a specific business need. The programme develops data science capabilities through project-based learning while embedding best practices such as Git and Reproducible Analytical Pipelines (RAPs). By working on real projects, participants develop their own data science skills whilst delivering valuable results for the healthcare system. 

> "It was really gratifying to watch the accelerator cohort present all their projects- and particularly impressive the breadth of technical expertise which they covered, across really diverse healthcare domains. With this being my third time mentoring, it’s clear how value adding this programme is for disseminating data science techniques across all three participating organisations." — Mia Noonan, Principal Data Scientist

The Celebration Event gave participants the chance to share the insights, outcomes and impact of their projects. It was brilliant to see the breadth of work delivered; demonstrating what can be achieved when people are given dedicated time, mentorship and the opportunity to learn by doing. You can read about a previous mentee’s experience on the program [here](https://nhsengland.github.io/datascience/articles/2026/06/11/20260611-ds-accelerator/).

![image](../images/blogs_images/2026-joint-conference/7203b654-fa01-4f33-a776-c97709d8464a.png)

The afternoon began with the first of the week's learning and development sessions, an AI agent breaking challenge. Teams attempted to break AI agents, putting their prompt engineering and adversarial thinking skills to the test to learn the limitations of current AI systems. If you’d like to experiment with breaking AI chatbots and learn about using agents safely, [this article](https://nhsengland.github.io/datascience/articles/2026/11/05/breaking-chatbots/) provides a write-up of the workshop and lessons learnt.

The day closed with the first Joint Project Carousel — a series of quickfire presentations from data scientists across all three organisations, covering topics from automating clinical coding and improving data quality to making better use of unstructured text. This was followed up by a chance to ask questions and discuss projects amongst the team.

## Day Two – Collaboration Across Organisations

Wednesday was the joint conference day proper, with two further Project Carousel sessions in the morning giving colleagues the chance to share how AI and data science are being used to tackle real-world challenges across the healthcare system. 

The afternoon featured a poster session, where attendees from all three organisations shared posters showcasing current and past projects. The posters spanned the breadth of data science work being undertaken, including predictive modelling, synthetic data, geospatial analysis, Natural Language Processing, user segmentation and AI tooling, with applications ranging from automating survey classification to modelling patient pathways. The poster session allowed for further discussion and collaboration between data scientists from the different organisations:

> "The variety of projects being presented from all organisations was impressive! Lots were incredibly interesting, and there was a general sense of excitement for future collaboration. For me personally, the poster session was particularly successful. Since presenting my poster on synthetic panels, other organisations have already reached out and asked for further meetings, learning more about my project and discussing how we can work together in the future." — Will Poulett, Senior Data Scientist

![9LIP3I3A](../images/blogs_images/2026-joint-conference/73acf00d-7e07-4a71-bec2-5921ed18eb52.jpg)

The day also included a workshop on Reusables, where colleagues shared ideas about how to reuse ideas, learning, and even modelling across teams and organisations. Collaboration, working across organisations to ensure work is delivered efficiently and in a joined-up manner was a theme that ran through the whole week.

> "I enjoyed seeing such a wide range of projects and work from the Data Science Accelerator, as well as projects from across NHS England, DHSC and NHS BSA. What I most enjoyed was the focus on not just sharing the work, but thinking (and documenting!) how we can then build on and reuse this work in different ways." — Adam Hollings, Principal Data Scientist

The conference was followed by an ‘If I had a day’ themed hackathon, where attendees worked with new AI solutions and methods which they had not yet had the time to explore; learning, developing, and sharing knowledge across organisations. You can read about the hackathon [here](https://nhsengland.github.io/datascience/articles/2026/11/03/ai_and_its_future_in_the_nhs_a_data_science_hackathon/).
